To qualify for the AR exam, candidates must meet one of the following requirements:

  • A Bachelor’s (BA or BS) or higher degree from an accredited college or university and 5 years of laboratory animal experience, of which at least 3 years is in management or supervision;
  • Associate's degree and 8 years of laboratory animal experience of which at least 3 years is in management or supervision;
  • High school/GED diploma and 10 years of laboratory animal experience of which at least 3 years is in management or supervision.

Examples of management or supervisory experience:

  • Hiring, firing, and/or direct supervision of employees
  • Administering performance appraisals
  • Managing facility resources
  • Managing a budget

ICPM requirements for the CM portion of the CMAR are different from AALAS requirements for the AR exam. Approval to sit for the CM portion of the exam does not automatically qualify you to sit for the AR portion.

Significant preparation must precede NHPs entering animal facilities. A full medical history review should be undertaken by trained and experienced veterinary and other relevant personnel prior to selecting an appropriate cohort of animals. The selection process may start months or even years in advance if significant work is required prior to moving the animals into biocontainment.

For example, animals on vaccine challenge studies may spend months being vaccinated before being introduced into the biocontainment facility for challenge with the infectious agent.

Once animals are selected for potential suitability for a project, they should undergo physical examinations, medical assessments, clinical chemistries, complete blood counts, serological screenings, prophylactic care, and other procedures as deemed necessary by the veterinarian. This can include the types of manipulations which may interfere with the research study or may not be able to be performed in biocontainment.

For example, tuberculosis testing, dental work, surgical procedures (such as implantation of medical devices), or imaging work should be performed at the lowest biocontainment housing level whenever possible.

Some animals may be found to be unsuitable for a research project during the pre-study screening, so generally a larger cohort group should be screened in case some animals are removed from consideration during this process.
